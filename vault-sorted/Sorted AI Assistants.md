## Initial repositioning & experience brief 

**Status:** Working direction for critique and iteration, not a final specification.

## 1. The decision

Sorted will prioritise **tailored, implemented AI assistants for businesses** as its flagship commercial offer. The customer buys a *working assistant with a job to do*, not access to another AI tool, a chatbot, or an open-ended automation project.

**Primary category:** AI assistants for business  
**Positioning:** **AI assistants that do the work.**  
**Purchase promise:** **We'll build yours and put it to work.**  
**Invitation:** **Tell us what you'd like your assistant to do.**  
**Proof:** **Try giving one a job.**

The ambition is not to be known for generic AI capability. It is to be known for the remarkably natural experience of acquiring useful operational capacity.

### Why now

AI models and automation tools are increasingly accessible. What remains difficult for many local businesses is translating those capabilities into a reliable assistant that understands *their* working practices, uses *their* channels and performs *their* jobs. Sorted takes responsibility for that adoption gap.

This is a **working market hypothesis** to validate with prospects, not a claim that the market is uncontested or that all competitors require manual implementation.

## 2. The North Star: what Boardy.ai taught us

The benchmark is not Boardy.ai 's exact product, network, pricing, or backend. It is its **continuity of experience**:

1. **One remarkable, instantly legible action.** “I can introduce you to anyone. Try me.” A buyer knows what to ask for.
2. **Immediate entry into the customer's environment.** The experience moved straight to text messaging instead of requiring the user to inhabit a new product dashboard.
3. **Natural escalation of media.** A live message thread became an AI phone conversation when richer context was useful, then an optional Google Meet conversation when a presentation made sense.
4. **Expectation-setting before unfamiliar behaviour.** Before calling, Boardy prepared the user for speaking with AI and described how the call would pause and flow. That made a new interaction less alien.
5. **Context continuity.** The assistant retained the thread's facts across messages and calls. The user did not have to restart their story.
6. **Clear commercial transaction.** For its paid introductions, Boardy described an outcome and a per-completed-meeting charge, rather than selling access to technical infrastructure.

**Sorted's design principle:** *The assistant should feel present in the customer's working world, not trapped inside Sorted's app.*

The goal is to model the coherence of this journey, **not** to copy Boardy's specific promises or pricing.

## 3. Who Sorted is for

**Primary initial audience:** Owner-operated and small local businesses who want the capabilities of a bigger team but lack the interest, time or technical confidence to configure AI tools themselves.

**Initial demonstration vertical:** Trades / local field-service businesses (to keep the first experience concrete and buildable). The underlying architecture should permit later sector-specific assistants, e.g. accountants, clinics, property services and training providers.

**Their desire:** “I'd love to have someone take care of that.”  
**Their concern:** “Do I have to learn software or change everything first?”  
**Sorted's answer:** “No. We'll learn how you work, tailor your assistant, connect what it needs, and get it doing the job.”

Do not require the buyer to understand APIs, agents, operator chains, CRMs, prompts, webhooks or workflow redesign before they can experience value.

## 4. The message architecture

### What must be legible in seconds

- **What is it?** An AI assistant for your business.
- **What does it do?** Real jobs: enquiries, quote preparation, booking, follow-ups and other defined work.
- **Why is it different?** It does the work inside agreed workflows, rather than merely suggesting how the owner could do it.
- **Who sets it up?** Sorted does.
- **How do I know?** Give the sample assistant a job before you book a meeting.

### Copy territory to test

**Option A — category-led (recommended starting baseline):**  
**AI assistants that do the work.**  
*Tell us what you'd like yours to do.*

**Option B — ownership-led:**  
**Your business can have an assistant now.**  
*We'll build it, teach it how you work and put it to work.*

**Option C — task-led campaign:**  
**Tell me about a job. I'll prepare the quote.**  
*Try a sample assistant. Then we'll tailor one to your business.*

**Do not lead with:** “Business modernisation”, “AI agents for every workflow”, “autonomous GTM systems”, “optimise your operations”, or a catalogue of unrelated automation possibilities.

Aspirational language is the invitation; **specific completed work is the proof**.

## 5. The experience we want to build

### Stage 0 — Discover

A landing page or social campaign makes a concise, buyable promise: **“AI assistants that do the work.”** The primary CTA is **“Try giving one a job”** (or a validated variant), not “Book a discovery call.”

### Stage 1 — Enter the user's channel

The visitor chooses a supported contact channel, ideally **SMS or WhatsApp** in the first release, after clear consent. The journey moves to that existing messaging environment quickly. Do not make a Sorted dashboard the mandatory place to experience the service.

The assistant introduces itself honestly as AI and asks **one relevant question**, not a long intake form: e.g. “What kind of business do you run?” Then: “What would you love having someone else handle?”

### Stage 2 — Understand one job

The assistant asks only enough questions to scope a **single demonstrable task**, using plain business language. For a trades prospect: enquiries, job details and a draft quote. It repeats the intended job and gives the user a chance to correct it.

**The customer should feel understood, not interrogated.**

### Stage 3 — Do a real sample task

The assistant invites a sample enquiry, voice note or job description. Within an isolated demo workspace it produces a meaningful result: a structured enquiry, missing-detail request, draft estimate based on explicit demo rules, or ready-to-approve follow-up. Demonstrate a **closed loop**, not just a clever reply.

The sample must clearly distinguish **demo data** from the customer's actual business operations. Never invent pricing rules or imply that a draft quote is authorised to send.

### Stage 4 — Escalate naturally to a call

When voice is genuinely better for discovery, the assistant **offers** to call. Before calling, it sets expectations in the spirit of the Boardy experience:

> “Want to talk it through? I'm an AI assistant. I can pause, listen and work through it with you. It may feel a little different from a normal call — would you like me to ring you?”

Call only with clear opt-in and supported telephony. Preserve what the user already said by text; don't restart the interview.

### Stage 5 — Show the tailored possibility

If the prospect wants to see how it would operate in *their* business, transition to a short walkthrough or screen-share/Google Meet **with context already captured**. A human from Sorted may join; this must be explicit rather than presented as fully autonomous if it isn't.

Deliver a concrete proposal: **job to be delegated → channels/data required → what the assistant can do → approval boundaries → installation scope → fee → go-live criteria.**

### Stage 6 — Install and activate

Sorted carries the setup burden: discovery, integrations, rule capture, test cases, approvals, deployment, monitoring and exception routing. The customer provides access, verifies business rules and approves deployment decisions. We minimise their technical burden; we do not promise zero customer involvement.

**North Star:** The buyer experiences *hiring help*, not *managing an IT project*.

## 6. First flagship demo: the job handoff

**Suggested initial vertical:** Local trades.  
**First demonstration job:** “Give me a customer enquiry; I'll take it to a prepared next action.”

**Demonstrated sequence:**

1. Prospect texts a realistic example enquiry to the Sorted demo number.
2. Assistant identifies the customer need and extracts the details.
3. Assistant asks for genuinely missing information.
4. Prospect replies (text or voice note).
5. Assistant creates an organised job record and, if configured demo rules allow, a **draft quote for approval**.
6. Assistant summarises: “Here's what I handled, what still needs your decision, and what happens next.”
7. The prospect is invited to tailor an assistant to their own business.

**End state:** The prospect sees a piece of work *completed or safely advanced*, not just a conversation.

**Pass/fail test:** Does a local business owner spontaneously ask “Can you put that in my business?”

## 7. What Sorted actually sells

**Customer-facing product:** A tailored, installed AI assistant that performs a defined job.  
**Service delivery:** Workflow discovery, configuration, integration, testing, supervision design and ongoing support.  
**Internal infrastructure:** Reusable communication adapters, operator chains, workflow state, business rules, approval gates, audit logs and exception handling.

The implementation work is real, but the marketing should not foreground its complexity.

### Guardrails for productisation

- Start with a **limited menu of supported jobs**, even if discovery language remains broad.
- Standardise the operating environment and supported integrations.
- Separate normal configuration from genuinely bespoke workflow redesign.
- Keep sensitive external actions behind suitable approval, permission and audit controls.
- Clearly distinguish **drafted**, **approved**, **sent**, **booked** and **paid** states.
- Do not use employee-equivalence, 24/7 reliability, time-savings or revenue claims without evidence.

## 8. Commercial model — to test, not announce yet

Potential structure:

- **Installation fee:** Scoped configuration, integration and acceptance testing.
- **Operating fee:** Continued execution, infrastructure, supervision and maintenance within defined usage.
- **Possible outcome component later:** Verified, attributable completed jobs (such as qualified bookings), only once measurement and dispute rules are robust.

**Do not lead the marketing with staffing cost comparisons.** Lead with the desirability and proof of having an assistant. Use cost/capacity comparisons as buying justification after the experience.

## 9. Homepage adjustment: not a complete redesign

Keep Sorted's existing premium brand language: confident typography, stark black/white composition, acid-lime highlights, strong visuals and straightforward copy. The goal is **experience-first repositioning**, not another aesthetic reinvention.

Suggested information hierarchy:

1. **Hero:** “AI assistants that do the work.” / “Tell us what you'd like yours to do.”
2. **Primary action:** Experience an assistant immediately via message; no obligatory consultation or platform account.
3. **Proof:** A very short real task-completion sequence with clear visual states.
4. **Jobs:** Enquiries, quotations, bookings, follow-ups — examples of work, not a software feature dump.
5. **How it becomes yours:** Meet → Tailor → Put to work.
6. **Trust:** Real implementation boundaries, approvals, privacy, support, and verified customer evidence once available.
7. **Secondary conversion:** Schedule a tailored walkthrough **after** prospective value is established.

Websites and advertising can remain adjacent Sorted capabilities or acquisition channels, but should no longer dilute the flagship AI-assistant narrative.

## 10. Content strategy: show the experience, not the machinery

**Narrative:** “The capabilities of a bigger business, without needing to become a bigger business first.”

Content formats to develop:

- **One job, one result:** Raw customer message → handled workflow → owner receives an actionable result.
- **Meet your assistant:** First-person, conversational walkthrough in familiar channels.
- **The modern local business:** Show a small team operating with administrative capacity that formerly required extra hands.
- **Behind the reliability:** Selective proof of how approvals, exceptions and business context make the assistant dependable — without leading with engineering jargon.

**The brand should feel intentionally built by people who understand businesses.** Avoid generic agent-avatar aesthetics, feature-sprawl, empty AI promises and chatbot-like demos disconnected from real outcomes.

## 11. Compounding advantage: context + trust

Once legitimately integrated, Sorted can accumulate authorised, useful **business-specific operating context**: terminology, workflows, approved rules, exceptions and historical state. That can make the assistant more relevant and make switching less attractive.

But the aim is **earned retention through usefulness**, not lock-in: customers retain clear ownership/control of their data, with appropriate access, export and deletion arrangements.

This is a possible advantage, not a substitute for demonstrable reliability or customer consent.

## 12. Relationship to ADX Engine

**Sorted:** Productised installation of assistants that perform specific jobs in small businesses. The customer buys an operating capability.  
**ADX Engine:** Bespoke workflow redesign and AI-enabled operating architecture for more complex organisations. The customer buys organisational redesign and implementation.

Common discipline: workflow understanding, decomposition, integration, verification and operational adoption. Different buying heuristic, complexity and delivery scope.

## 13. MVP acceptance criteria

A first release should be considered successful only if:

- A new visitor can understand the offer and begin a demo without creating a software account or booking a sales meeting.
- A real SMS/WhatsApp interaction (where supported) retains context through the sample workflow.
- One realistic business task reaches a **clear result**, using actual system execution rather than manually staged responses.
- The assistant can safely ask for clarification, refuse an unsupported action, and escalate to a human.
- The prospect can opt into a voice call with accurate expectation-setting; the conversation does not restart from zero.
- The handoff to a human walkthrough includes a coherent context summary.
- The seller can clearly explain what is included in installation, what isn't, and what the buyer must authorise.
- We can measure demo starts, task completions, requested walkthroughs, qualified opportunities and paid installs.

## 14. Immediate build priorities

**Priority 1 — Promise and first job:** Freeze one headline and one demonstrable workflow.  
**Priority 2 — Messaging entry:** Build opt-in entry into a real communication channel, with consent, identity and context capture.  
**Priority 3 — Working loop:** Build one reliable input → decision → action → result chain with an owner-approval boundary.  
**Priority 4 — Media escalation:** Add optional AI phone interaction and context-preserving handoff to a human walkthrough.  
**Priority 5 — Launch content:** Capture the real experience in ads and short videos. Test aspiration + demonstration against generic automation messaging.

### Explicitly out of scope for v0.1

- An unlimited “build any agent” promise.
- A giant library of industry templates before the first workflow works.
- Requiring customers to adopt a new dashboard to experience the product.
- Fully autonomous sending of unapproved estimates or financial commitments.
- A complete homepage rebuild before the messaging demonstration is validated.

## 15. Open decisions for the next iteration

1. Which job is the first **complete** demonstration: enquiry qualification, quote drafting, booking, or follow-up?
2. Which channel launches first: SMS, WhatsApp or a choice of both? What are the permission and provider constraints?
3. How will the assistant maintain identity and consent across text, call and walkthrough?
4. When should an AI phone call be offered rather than a human call?
5. What standard data/workflow environment does Sorted own, and what must be connected from the client?
6. What exact acceptance tests define “installed and working”?
7. What is the minimum profitable implementation and operating price?

---

### Governing principle

> **Don't make a business owner learn how to buy AI automation. Let them give an assistant a job, experience the result, and then put one to work in their business.**

**Internal shorthand:** *Promise → Message → Understand → Do → Call → Show → Install → Work.*
