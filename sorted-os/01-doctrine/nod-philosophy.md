# Nod Philosophy

The Nod system breaks an engagement into approval gates. Each gate asks the client to approve something real and reduces the need for a leap of faith. Its deeper purpose is to turn an uncertain service transaction into an informed product transaction.

| Nod | Approval | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Nod 1 | High-fidelity mockup | Vision and direction approved |
| Nod 2 | Working static build | Execution approved; Stage 2 CMS may begin |
| Nod 3 | Quote | Price and delivery terms approved |
| Nod 4 | Payment | Delivery, CMS access, and handoff unlocked |

SortedUpdates is deliberately applied after Nod 2. The client evaluates the approved design as a clean static site; the CMS is a delivery mechanism, not a feature that complicates product evaluation.

## What the Nod Protects

Before Nod 1, Sorted carries the manufacturing risk. The prospect owes nothing and has not bought creative hours, revisions, or a claim on production capacity.

At the Nod, the prospect is saying: **"Yes. I want this website."** The object of exchange is known to both sides. The customer is protected from paying for an unknown outcome; Sorted is protected from an undefined creative obligation.

The commercial sequence is:

```
Build -> Inspect -> Nod -> Fixed Price -> Acquire -> Content Setup -> QA -> Launch
```

There is no revision stage. Factual corrections are part of launch readiness. A request to rework structure, composition, colour direction, page types, or component behaviour is customisation: new work, separately scoped.
