# Human Psychology

## Purpose

Human Psychology is the highest-order doctrine within the Sorted Design Language.

Every design decision made by the Site Composer exists to satisfy human perception before visual preference.

Users do not consciously analyse websites.

They instinctively judge them.

This document defines those instincts.

Every lower-order doctrine inherits from these rules.

---

# Core Principle

People do not trust because they understand.

People understand because they first trust.

Trust always precedes comprehension.

A website therefore has one responsibility before any other:

Create confidence.

Only afterwards should it explain.

---

# Rule 01

## The Three Second Judgement

Visitors determine within seconds whether a business appears:

- legitimate
- professional
- competent
- current
- trustworthy

This judgement happens before reading.

Visual appearance therefore carries greater weight than copy during first contact.

Design must optimise this first impression.

---

# Rule 02

## Reduce Uncertainty

Every section should reduce one uncertainty.

Examples:

Can they solve my problem?

Do they specialise?

Will they answer?

Are they local?

Have others trusted them?

What happens next?

Each section should remove one question from the customer's mind.

---

# Rule 03

## One Dominant Decision

Humans struggle when multiple decisions compete equally.

Each viewport should communicate:

One message.

One action.

One dominant image.

One obvious reading path.

Never ask visitors to decide where to look.

---

# Rule 04

## Scanning Before Reading

Visitors scan.

They do not read.

Hierarchy should therefore communicate meaning before text is consumed.

Design should support:

- large headlines
- obvious grouping
- strong whitespace
- visual rhythm
- short supporting paragraphs

---

# Rule 05

## Photography Creates Belief

Photography communicates evidence.

Copy communicates explanation.

Images should prove.

Words should reinforce.

Photography should demonstrate:

- competence
- professionalism
- humanity
- confidence
- quality

Avoid decorative imagery.

Every image should increase trust.

---

# Rule 06

## Familiarity Reduces Cognitive Load

People trust interfaces they understand.

Novelty should never compromise clarity.

Innovation should occur within familiar interaction patterns.

The interface should disappear.

The business should remain.

---

# Rule 07

## Progressive Trust

Trust compounds.

It should never exist in one section.

Trust should accumulate throughout the experience.

Example:

Hero

↓

Services

↓

Social Proof

↓

Process

↓

Guarantees

↓

CTA

Every section should slightly increase confidence.

---

# Rule 08

## Simplicity Signals Confidence

Businesses that appear organised feel more competent.

Remove unnecessary elements.

Avoid visual noise.

Every element should justify its existence.

---

# Rule 09

## Consistency Creates Professionalism

Consistency creates subconscious confidence.

Consistency includes:

spacing

typography

colour

motion

buttons

cards

icons

interaction

Repeated patterns reduce cognitive effort.

---

# Rule 10

## Every Decision Serves Trust

Every design decision should answer one question:

Does this increase trust?

If not,

remove it.
