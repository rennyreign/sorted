# Hero Component

## Purpose

The Hero creates emotional certainty.

It is the highest-value viewport on the entire site.

The visitor should leave the hero knowing:

- This business understands my problem.
- This business can solve it.
- I can trust them.
- I know what to do next.

---

# Inputs

Industry.

Brand personality.

Emotional positioning.

Photography doctrine.

Copy.

Primary CTA.

Trust signal.

---

# Required content

The hero must contain four elements, not just three:

1. **One emotional promise** — the headline that names the customer's desired outcome
2. **One proof** — a visible reason to believe (work photo, team, result, stat, or years)
3. **One reassurance** — a trust cue that removes anxiety (24/7, local, fast response, guarantee)
4. **One action** — the primary CTA, clear and immediate

Not simply:

Headline.

Paragraph.

CTA.

---

# Required Assets

Hero image.

Headline.

Supporting statement.

CTA.

Trust indicator.

---

# Hero dominance

The hero must be the most visually dominant section on the page, or tied only with the final CTA.

Ways to create dominance:

- Full viewport height (100dvh) or near-full viewport (90dvh)
- Large hero image that occupies 50–60% of the visual weight
- Headline that is the largest type on the page by a clear margin
- Strong contrast: dark background with light text, or light background with dark bold type
- Minimal surrounding elements: one headline, one statement, one CTA, one image

The hero should not compete with the trust strip, services, or any other section for attention.

---

# Hero layout variants

### Split hero (text left, image right)

Best for: utility, trade, local services

- Text side: 40–45% of viewport
- Image side: 55–60% of viewport
- Image should be the dominant visual element
- Text should be concise and vertically centred

### Full-bleed hero (text over image)

Best for: lifestyle, premium, hospitality, editorial

- Image covers the full viewport
- Text sits over a dark overlay
- Single centred message
- Strongest emotional impact

### Utility hero (text + contact card)

Best for: emergency trades, fast response services

- Text and CTA on the left
- A visible phone number or contact card on the right
- Image may be smaller or absent if contact urgency is more important
- Trust badges sit directly beneath the headline

---

# Psychological Goal

Reduce uncertainty.

Increase confidence.

Create emotional certainty.

Encourage continuation.

---

# Failure Conditions

Competing CTAs.

Multiple messages.

Decorative photography.

Weak hierarchy.

Hero that does not dominate the page.

Large feature lists.

Hero taller than necessary.

Missing trust cue.

Missing proof.

Headline that describes the business instead of the outcome.

Text and image competing for equal importance.

---

# Validation

Can a visitor answer:

Who are they?

What do they do?

Can I trust them?

What should I do next?

What reassurance do I have?

Without scrolling?

Does the hero feel like the most important section on the page?

If not,

the Hero has failed.
