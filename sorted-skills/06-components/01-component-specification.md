# Component Specification

## Philosophy

Components are not visual blocks.

Components are behavioural operators.

Each component exists to solve one psychological problem.

Not one layout problem.

---

Every component should define:

Purpose

Inputs

Outputs

Trust Contribution

Required Assets

Copy Requirements

Photography Requirements

Composition Rules

Sizing Rules

Failure Conditions

Validation Rules

Never create components without purpose.

Purpose precedes implementation.

# Component sizing rules

## When to use 2 cards vs 3 cards vs 4 cards

Use **2 cards** when:

- The services are distinct and premium
- Each card needs a large image
- The story calls for contrast, not breadth
- The business has 2–3 core offerings

Use **3 cards** when:

- The business has 3 clear service categories
- The section is medium intensity, not the climax
- Each card can be scanned quickly

Use **4 cards** only when:

- The service list is genuinely long
- The cards are small and icon-led
- The section is low intensity

Never use more than 4 cards in a single row.

## When to use a featured card vs equal cards

Use a **featured card** (one large, others smaller) when:

- One service or result is the primary offering
- The story needs a clear focal point
- The featured item carries emotional weight

Use **equal cards** when:

- All items are equally important
- The visitor is comparing options
- The section is functional, not emotional

## When to use a large image block vs a grid of small images

Use a **large image block** when:

- The image is proof of a result
- The story calls for a moment of impact
- The image should dominate the viewport

Use a **grid of small images** when:

- Variety is the point
- The section is low intensity
- The visitor is browsing examples

## When to use a featured testimonial vs equal testimonial cards

Use a **featured testimonial** (one large quote, others supporting) when:

- One review is particularly strong
- The story calls for a confidence moment
- The testimonial is the proof section

Use **equal testimonial cards** when:

- All reviews are similar in weight
- The section is compressed and functional
- The goal is breadth, not depth

## When to use a split section vs a centred section

Use a **split section** (text left, image right) when:

- Both text and image are equally important
- The image proves the text
- The section is medium intensity

Use a **centred section** when:

- The message is simple and emotional
- The section is a statement, not a proof
- The section is low or high intensity, not medium

Use a **full-bleed image section** when:

- The image is the message
- The section is a proof moment
- The story calls for visual impact

# Sizing hierarchy rule

Every section should be sized relative to its story role:

| Story role | Typical size | Layout |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | Massive | Full viewport or dominant split |
| Trust Strip | Compressed | Horizontal strip or badge row |
| Services | Medium | 2–3 cards or featured + supporting |
| Work / Proof | Large | Full-bleed image or large split |
| Process | Compressed | Numbered steps or icon row |
| About | Medium | Split or large image + text |
| Testimonials | Proof | Featured quote or 2–3 cards |
| CTA | Decision | Full-width band, dark or bold |

No two adjacent sections should share the same size.
