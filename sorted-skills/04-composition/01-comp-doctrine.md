# Composition Doctrine

## Purpose

Composition governs how visual elements occupy space.

It determines:

- what dominates
- what supports
- what disappears

Composition exists independently of colour, typography or components.

It is the architecture of attention.

---

# Core Philosophy

Good composition should feel inevitable.

Visitors should never consciously think about layout.

Their attention should naturally arrive at the intended destination.

If the visitor has to search,

composition has failed.

---

# Primary Objective

Every viewport should answer three questions instantly.

1.

Where do I look?

2.

What is this?

3.

What should I do next?

Nothing should compete with those answers.

---

# Composition Order

Every viewport should follow:

Primary

↓

Supporting

↓

Evidence

↓

Action

↓

Continuation

Never reverse this order.

---

# Rule 01

One dominant focal point.

Never create two equally important elements.

One always wins.

---

# Rule 02

Composition should remove decisions.

The eye should travel automatically.

Never require exploration.

---

# Rule 03

Negative space is structural.

Whitespace is not decoration.

Whitespace creates hierarchy.

---

# Rule 04

Every composition should create confidence before complexity.

Simple first.

Detailed later.

---

# Rule 05

Composition should feel calm.

Avoid visual anxiety.

Avoid dense arrangements.

Avoid excessive decoration.

---

# Validation

Close your eyes.

Open them for one second.

If the primary message is not obvious,

recompose.
