# Viewport Mathematics

## Purpose

The viewport is a finite attention container.

Every pixel has opportunity cost.

Space should be allocated intentionally.

---

# Visual Weight

Every viewport should approximately allocate:

50%

Primary experience

30%

Supporting information

20%

Action

These ratios may vary slightly by industry.

---

# Above the Fold

Should communicate:

Identity

↓

Trust

↓

Action

Nothing else.

---

# Component Weight

Every component has weight.

Large image

High weight.

Headline

High weight.

Paragraph

Low weight.

Divider

Very low weight.

Weight should reduce as the eye travels downward.

---

# White Space Ratio

Whitespace should increase around:

Headlines

Photography

CTA

Testimonials

Never compress decision points.

---

# Visual surprise

Every page should contain one memorable viewport.

Not every section should have equal importance.

Visual surprise creates the moment a visitor remembers and trusts.

Ways to create visual surprise:

- One section that breaks the expected rhythm
- One oversized image that dominates the viewport
- One unexpected section background shift
- One bold typographic moment
- One asymmetrical layout among symmetrical sections
- One section that uses negative space dramatically

The surprise should still serve the message. Never surprise purely for decoration.

For a service business, the memorable viewport is often:

- the hero
- a large work-in-progress image
- a dramatic before/after or result section
- a powerful testimonial with large type

---

# Validation

Does the viewport feel balanced?

Or crowded?

Balance wins.

Does the page contain one memorable viewport?

If every section feels the same,

introduce a moment of intentional contrast.
