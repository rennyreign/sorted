# Storytelling Rhythm

## Purpose

Hierarchy controls where the eye goes.

Storytelling rhythm controls how the visitor feels along the way.

A page should move the visitor through a psychological arc:

```
Beginning

↓

Curiosity

↓

Evidence

↓

Confidence

↓

Commitment

↓

Closure
```

---

## Core principle

Every section is a chapter.

Each chapter must leave the visitor ready for the next one.

---

## The arc

### Beginning

The visitor arrives with a problem.

The hero must name that problem and promise a resolution.

Example: "Local plumbing help, any time of day."

### Curiosity

The visitor wants to know if this business can help them.

The Trust Strip or Services section should make them lean in.

Example: "24/7 emergency response. Usually under 1 hour."

### Evidence

The visitor needs proof.

Show work, process, results, or real people.

Example: a large image of a plumber at work, or a before/after result.

### Confidence

The visitor starts to believe.

Testimonials, guarantees, and credentials build trust.

Example: customer reviews with locations and outcomes.

### Commitment

The visitor decides to act.

The CTA section removes the final friction.

Example: "Call now. We are ready to help."

### Closure

The visitor knows what happens next.

The footer confirms contact details, location, and professionalism.

---

## Service storyboard

For a trade or service business, the scroll should feel like a short movie of the customer experience:

```
Plumber receives call
↓
Travels to home
↓
Diagnoses the problem
↓
Repairs the issue
↓
Customer is relieved
↓
Reviews the work
↓
Phone rings again
```

Map this story to sections:

| Story beat | Section | Visual |
|---|---|---|
| Call | Hero | Plumber on phone or van, ready |
| Travel | Trust Strip | Local coverage, response time |
| Diagnosis | Services | Identifying the problem (emergency, leak, boiler) |
| Repair | Work / Gallery | Close-up of hands fixing the problem |
| Relief | About | Customer home restored, plumber finishing |
| Reviews | Testimonials | Happy customer, quote |
| Next call | CTA | Phone ringing, invitation to call |

This is not a brochure. It is a story of transformation.

---

## Section roles

Map each section to a role in the story:

| Section | Story role | Emotional purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | Beginning | relief, hope, recognition |
| Trust Strip | Curiosity | reassurance, safety |
| Services | Diagnosis | understanding, capability |
| Work / Gallery | Repair | proof, competence |
| Process | Method | predictability, trust |
| About | Result | humanity, credibility |
| Testimonials | Confidence | social proof, belief |
| CTA | Commitment | action, safety |
| Footer | Closure | stability, contact |

---

## Pacing rules

- Never tell the same story twice in adjacent sections.
- Alternate between emotional and practical sections.
- If a section does not move the arc forward, remove it.
- The hero should create tension (the problem). The CTA should release it (the solution).
- The story should feel like a movie, not a brochure.

---

## Validation

Read the page from top to bottom. Does the emotional journey feel like:

"I have a problem → maybe these people can help → they look capable → others trust them → I will call them."

If the page jumps from problem to proof without curiosity, or from proof to footer without commitment, the rhythm is broken.
