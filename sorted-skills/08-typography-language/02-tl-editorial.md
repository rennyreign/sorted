# Editorial Typography Language

## Purpose

Editorial typography communicates quality, confidence, and intellectual authority.

It resembles a premium magazine or journal.

This typography language is used for architecture, interior design, professional coaching, private healthcare, photographers, and premium professional services.

---

## Core principle

Typography carries the design.

Colour and decoration should recede. Type should lead.

---

## Font stack

- **Display / headings:** A strong serif or high-contrast sans-serif
  - Recommended: Playfair Display, Canela, or a refined sans-serif like Söhne or Neue Montreal
- **Body:** A clean, highly legible sans-serif or serif
  - Recommended: Source Serif 4, Merriweather, or Inter
- **Mono / labels:** A light mono for captions, metadata, and small labels

Use **max three weights** per page: regular, medium, and bold.

---

## Type scale

- **Hero H1:** `clamp(3.5rem, 9vw, 8.5rem)` — tight leading, bold or medium weight
- **Section H2:** `clamp(2.25rem, 5vw, 4rem)` — medium or semibold, tight leading
- **Card H3:** `text-xl to text-2xl` — medium weight
- **Body:** `text-base to text-lg leading-relaxed` — regular weight
- **Pull quote / large text:** `text-2xl to text-4xl` — medium or regular, generous line height
- **Labels / captions:** `text-xs uppercase tracking-[0.15em]` — regular or medium

---

## Rules

### Headlines

- Headlines can be longer and more expressive than utility type.
- Allow tight leading, but never let descenders crash into ascenders.
- Use generous negative letter-spacing on very large headlines.
- Avoid all caps. Sentence case or title case only.

### Body

- Body should feel calm and readable.
- Use `max-w-[70ch]` for editorial reading length.
- Slightly larger body size (`text-lg`) is acceptable for premium readability.

### Contrast

- Create strong scale contrast between hero and body.
- Use weight changes to create hierarchy, not colour changes.
- Subheadings should be clearly lighter than headlines but not too small.

### Quotes

- Testimonials and pull quotes should use larger type with tighter leading.
- Consider a different weight or style for quotes to make them feel designed.

---

## Hierarchy

1. Hero headline — dominant, oversized, maybe serif
2. Pull quote / large statement — secondary focal point
3. Section headline — medium scale, calm
4. Body — readable, generous
5. Caption / label — small, tracked, functional

---

## Validation

Does the typography feel like it was chosen by a designer, or like a default?

If it feels like a default, the scale is too small or the weights are too similar.
