# Luxury Typography Language

## Purpose

Luxury typography communicates exclusivity, refinement, and desire.

It should feel expensive and unhurried.

This typography language is used for luxury beauty, hospitality, high-end fitness, luxury trades, premium real estate, and boutique professional services.

---

## Core principle

Luxury is expressed through restraint and precision.

Less type. More space. More confidence.

---

## Font stack

- **Display / headings:** A refined serif or elegant sans-serif
  - Recommended: Cinzel, Cormorant Garamond, or a custom-feeling sans-serif like Freight Display
- **Body:** A clean, classic sans-serif or humanist serif
  - Recommended: Inter, Canela, or Source Serif 4
- **Labels / captions:** A small, wide-tracked uppercase sans-serif

Use **max two weights** per page: regular and medium, or light and regular. Avoid bold unless it is a deliberate feature.

---

## Type scale

- **Hero H1:** `clamp(3rem, 8vw, 7.5rem)` — light or regular weight, very tight leading
- **Section H2:** `clamp(2rem, 4vw, 3.5rem)` — light or regular, generous spacing
- **Card H3:** `text-lg to text-xl` — regular or medium
- **Body:** `text-base leading-relaxed` — regular, slightly generous line height
- **Labels / eyebrows:** `text-xs uppercase tracking-[0.2em]` — wide tracking, generous letter spacing
- **Quotes:** `text-2xl to text-4xl` — light or regular, elegant

---

## Rules

### Headlines

- Headlines should feel spacious and considered.
- Use light or regular weights, not bold.
- Tight leading creates elegance; wide leading creates calm.
- Never use all caps for large headlines. Title case only.

### Body

- Body copy should be minimal.
- Use generous line height and comfortable measure.
- Avoid dense paragraphs. Luxury breathes.

### Labels

- Small labels should be uppercase with wide tracking.
- Use labels sparingly. They should feel like editorial markers, not badges.

### Numbers

- Stats and prices should be elegant, not loud.
- Use proportional figures if possible.
- Avoid large bold numbers unless they are a deliberate feature.

---

## Hierarchy

1. Hero headline — light, confident, dominant
2. Feature quote or large statement — elegant, secondary
3. Section headline — calm, spaced
4. Body — minimal, refined
5. Labels — small, uppercase, wide

---

## Validation

Does the typography feel expensive because of restraint, or because of ornament?

If ornament, remove it.
