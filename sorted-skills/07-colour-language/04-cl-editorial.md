# Editorial Colour Language

## Purpose

Editorial colour communicates restraint, confidence, and intellectual quality.

It resembles a premium magazine or journal rather than a software product.

This colour language is used for architecture, interior design, professional coaching, private healthcare, photographers, and premium professional services.

---

## Core principle

Colour should recede.

Typography, photography, and whitespace carry the design. Colour only provides structure.

---

## Palette structure

### Backgrounds

- **Primary background:** pure white or near-white (#FFFFFF or #FDFDFD)
- **Secondary background:** very light grey or warm paper (#F5F5F5 or #F7F5F2)
- **Dark background:** soft black or charcoal (#0A0A0A or #1A1A1A)
- **Feature background:** one muted accent used only for a single statement section

### Text

- **Primary text:** pure black or soft black (#0A0A0A or #1A1A1A)
- **Secondary text:** cool grey (#6B7280)
- **Inverse text:** white (#FFFFFF)

### Accent

- **Primary accent:** one restrained, sophisticated colour
  - Architecture: charcoal or steel blue (#3D4F60 or #4A5D6E)
  - Editorial / coaching: deep burgundy or forest green (#6B2D2D or #2F4F4F)
  - Photography / creative: warm black or ochre (#1A1A1A or #B8860B)
  - Healthcare: muted teal or navy (#3E6B7A or #1E3A5F)
- **Accent hover:** a darker, more saturated version
- **Use only one accent colour per site**

### Functional colours

- **Success:** muted green (#4A7C59)
- **Warning:** warm ochre (#B8860B)
- **Error:** deep rose (#9B4B4B)

---

## Colour usage rules

### Backgrounds

- White should dominate.
- Dark sections should be used for one high-impact moment.
- Never use mid-tone backgrounds unless absolutely necessary.

### Accents

- Accent colour should appear very sparingly:
  - primary button
  - one small detail per section
  - active state underline
  - a single quote or editorial marker
- Never use accent for headings.
- Never use accent for backgrounds except in one deliberate feature section.

### Contrast

- Maintain high contrast between text and background.
- Use black on white, white on black, and grey only for secondary text.
- Avoid coloured text on coloured backgrounds.

---

## Psychological purpose

| Colour | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Pure white | clarity, precision, space |
| Light grey | structure, quiet rhythm |
| Soft black | authority, sophistication |
| Restrained accent | intention, taste, focus |

---

## Validation

Does the colour palette feel like a decision was made, or does it feel like a default?

If it feels like a default, remove one colour.
