# Premium Colour Language

## Purpose

Premium colour communicates quality, confidence, and exclusivity.

It should feel expensive without being loud.

This colour language is used for beauty, hospitality, professional services, luxury trades, and high-end fitness.

---

## Core principle

Premium colour is restrained.

Impact comes from contrast, depth, and material quality, not from bright colours.

---

## Palette structure

### Backgrounds

- **Primary background:** warm white or soft cream (#FDFBF7 or #FAFAF9)
- **Secondary background:** light taupe or cool grey (#F2F0EB or #F5F5F4)
- **Dark background:** deep charcoal, navy, or espresso (#1C1917 or #0F172A)
- **Accent background:** a muted feature colour used only for one hero or CTA moment

### Text

- **Primary text:** warm near-black (#1C1917)
- **Secondary text:** warm grey (#78716C)
- **Inverse text:** warm white (#FAFAF9)

### Accent

- **Primary accent:** one refined colour
  - Luxury: deep navy (#1E3A5F) or gold (#C5A059)
  - Beauty: rose or mauve (#BE4B4B or #9B6A8E)
  - Hospitality: terracotta or sage (#C06C4F or #7A8B6E)
  - Professional: forest green or burgundy (#3F6B4F or #7B2D2D)
- **Accent hover:** a deeper, more saturated version of the same hue
- **Use only one accent colour per site**

### Functional colours

- **Success:** muted sage (#6B8E6E)
- **Warning:** warm amber (#D4A017)
- **Error:** deep rose (#B54A4A)

---

## Colour usage rules

### Backgrounds

- Warm neutrals should dominate.
- Dark sections should feel deliberate and expensive, not heavy.
- One section may use a feature accent as a background if it is the hero or a premium statement.

### Accents

- Accent colour should appear on:
  - primary buttons
  - subtle underline details
  - one icon or small shape per section
  - quote marks or editorial markers
- Never use accent for large headings unless it is a deliberate feature moment.
- Never use more than one accent on the same element.

### Depth

- Use subtle gradients from light to slightly darker neutral.
- Use shadows with warm tones, not cold grey.
- Use tonal variation within the same hue family to create depth.

---

## Psychological purpose

| Colour | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Warm white | cleanliness, calm, luxury |
| Taupe / grey | structure, restraint, maturity |
| Deep neutral | authority, confidence, focus |
| Refined accent | taste, intention, quality |

---

## Validation

Does the colour palette feel expensive because of restraint?

If it feels expensive because of decoration, remove something.
