# Skill: Outreach Drafter

**When to use:** When asked to draft a cold outreach email to a prospect after their site has been analysed by the Website Analyser. Requires a site score and outreach angle.

---

## What this skill does

Drafts a short, direct cold outreach email from Sorted to a local business prospect. The email references their specific site problems and leads with the redesign — "we already built it" not "we could build it."

---

## Inputs required

Before drafting, you need:
- Business name
- Business type (barber, restaurant, plumber, etc.)
- `outreach_angle` from the Website Analyser output
- `site_weaknesses` list (used to add specific detail)
- `site_score` (to calibrate urgency/tone)
- Whether a mockup has been generated (affects the email's core claim)

---

## Sorted's outreach model

Sorted's approach is "we show, we don't sell." The email must:

1. **Reference something specific** — a real problem they'll recognise. Not a generic pitch.
2. **Lead with the work done** — if a mockup exists, "we've already redesigned your homepage" is the hook
3. **Frame the business pain as enquiry leakage** — more of the people who already find them should be taking the next step.
4. **Be short** — 4–6 sentences maximum. Local business owners don't read long emails.
5. **Sound human** — like it was written by a person who actually looked at their site, not a mail merge
6. **Have one clear ask** — "want to see it?" or "can I send it over?" — not a pitch deck

---

## Email structure

```
Subject: [Business name] — we redesigned your website

Hi [first name if known, otherwise skip],

[Opening — specific reference to their site problem. One sentence.]

[The offer — "We've put together a new version / We redesigned your homepage."]

[One specific improvement — what the new version does that theirs doesn't.]

[The ask — short, low-friction. "Want to see it?" or "Happy to send it over if useful."]

[Sign-off]
Renaldo
Sorted
```

---

## Tone rules

**Do:** Direct. Specific. Conversational. Brief. Local. Confident without being pushy.

**Don't:**
- Use "elevate", "transform", "seamless", "next-gen", "cutting-edge", "innovative"
- Mention "digital presence" or "online visibility"
- Lead with "trust" as the main problem. Many good local businesses are already trusted; the sharper angle is whether the site converts enough visitors into enquiries.
- Start with "I hope this email finds you well"
- Include a long list of services or features
- Attach anything — the ask is just "want to see it?"
- Sound like a mass email campaign

## Example outputs

**Score 2/10 — hair salon, no CTA, broken mobile:**

> Subject: Priya's Hair Studio — we redesigned your website
>
> Hi Priya,
>
> We were looking at your site and noticed there's no way for new customers to book online — they'd have to call and hope someone picks up.
>
> We put together a redesigned version of your homepage that puts the booking link front and centre.
>
> Want to take a look?
>
> Renaldo
> Sorted

---

**Score 4/10 — plumber, exists but generic:**

> Subject: Fast Flow Plumbing — new website design
>
> Hi,
>
> Your current site doesn't show any customer reviews or photos of your work — which is exactly what convinces someone to call rather than go with the next plumber on Google.
>
> We've built you an updated version that leads with your reviews and has a clear call button in the header.
>
> Happy to send it over if you'd like to see it.
>
> Renaldo
> Sorted

---

## If no mockup exists yet

If a mockup hasn't been generated, adjust the offer:

> "We've put together some initial ideas for your homepage — want to see what we had in mind?"

Do not claim a full redesign is ready if it isn't.

---

## Output

Provide:
1. The draft email (subject + body)
2. One sentence note on what was personalised (so Renaldo can verify the specific reference is accurate)
3. Optional: one alternative subject line

Do not over-explain. Just give the email and the brief note.
