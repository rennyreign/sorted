About Kayleigh Dupont-Modeste

I’ve always been fascinated by people.

Not simply by personality, but by the invisible patterns that shape how we think, communicate, build relationships, make decisions, and understand ourselves.

My journey began long before I started consulting. While studying linguistics at university, I became fascinated by the psychology of language and the work of Noam Chomsky. I found myself asking questions that extended far beyond words themselves: Why do people communicate so differently? Why do two people hear the same conversation and leave with completely different interpretations? What drives the patterns beneath our behaviour?

Those questions eventually led me into counselling, personality psychology, and the work of thinkers including Carl Jung, John Beebe, Linda Berens, Dario Nardi, Catherine Stothart, Jean Shinoda Bolen, and C.S. Joseph. Their work invited me to frameworks to better understand the complexity of human personality—but it was working with real people that taught me how those ideas come alive.

Since 2019, I have worked with clients across the United Kingdom, the United States, Hong Kong, Egypt, Spain, Germany, France, Malaysia, Indonesia and beyond. During that time, I have profiled hundreds of individuals and worked in depth with dozens of clients through long-term consultations.

My work isn’t about placing people into boxes or assigning labels.

It’s about helping people understand themselves with greater clarity.

Every consultation begins with conversation. I listen carefully, allowing clients to speak freely before exploring the questions that matter most to them. Whether someone wants to understand their relationships, career, family dynamics, communication style, or personal growth, my role is to facilitate deeper self-understanding through evidence-based personality frameworks and thoughtful dialogue.

Over the years, I’ve watched clients make extraordinary life decisions with greater confidence. They’ve built successful careers, started businesses, strengthened relationships, relocated across countries, embraced new opportunities, and developed a clearer sense of who they are. While every journey is different, the common thread is clarity—and clarity often becomes the foundation for meaningful change.

Alongside my consulting work, I have applied personality theory within organisations, supporting recruitment, team communication, leadership development, and workplace compatibility. These experiences reinforced my belief that understanding personality isn’t only valuable for individuals—it transforms teams, workplaces, and relationships as well.

Today, I continuously pursue my academic interest higher education, with the long-term vision of building a consultancy brand that combines education, practitioner training, and personality consultation. My goal is to create a place where people can better understand themselves, understand one another, and use that understanding to build healthier relationships, stronger organisations, and more meaningful lives.

Because when we understand ourselves, we begin to understand everything else a little more clearly.

Revised About Kayleigh section

About Kayleigh

Personality is not a label. 

Kayleigh Dupont-Modeste is the founder of The Personality Company and an applied personality consultant specialising in Jungian typology, cognitive processes, communication patterns and interpersonal dynamics.

Since 2019, she has profiled several hundred individuals and worked with clients across the United Kingdom, the United States, Hong Kong, Dubai, Egypt, Germany, France, Malaysia and beyond.

Her work examines how people perceive information, organise experience, form judgments, communicate meaning and respond to psychological difference. Rather than treating personality type as a collection of surface traits, Kayleigh studies the underlying cognitive structure from which recurring behaviours and relational patterns emerge.

Her background in linguistics shaped this approach. An early interest in the relationship between language, cognition and unconscious structure developed into a broader study of psychological type and human behaviour. This allows her to listen not only to what a client says, but to how meaning is framed, what distinctions repeatedly appear and which patterns organise the client’s account of themselves.

Consultations are discursive rather than formulaic. Clients are given room to speak freely before the conversation is gradually organised around the questions, contradictions and recurring themes most relevant to them.

The objective is not to manufacture a tidy four-letter answer. Human beings are rarely that cooperative.

The objective is to produce a more precise account of how the individual thinks, communicates, interprets others and repeatedly approaches significant decisions.

Kayleigh has also applied personality analysis within commercial environments, including recruitment, talent management, team placement, professional development and organisational communication. Her work has supported individuals making substantial decisions concerning career progression, entrepreneurship, relocation, identity and long-term direction.

Alternative, more editorial version

Understanding personality requires more than recognising traits.

Many descriptions of personality begin at the visible level: habits, preferences, sociability, organisation or emotional expression.

Kayleigh’s work begins underneath that level.

She examines the cognitive patterns through which individuals attend to information, interpret experience, construct meaning and reach conclusions. These patterns help explain why two people may encounter the same situation yet perceive entirely different realities.

Since 2019, Kayleigh has worked internationally with several hundred individuals, using Jungian personality theory, behavioural observation, linguistic analysis and extended conversation to develop a more nuanced understanding of personality.

Her consultations are not tests with predetermined conclusions. They are structured investigations.

Clients speak freely, identify the questions they most want to explore and examine how recurring patterns appear across relationships, career choices, communication and personal development.

The work is rigorous, but not solemn. Personality is serious enough without making it unbearably serious.
