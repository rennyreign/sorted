## 1. GCSE Maths

### Short

**GCSE Maths gets much harder when the basics underneath it aren't secure.**

The difficulty is that students often revise what they already know. It feels productive, while the topics quietly costing them marks keep getting avoided.

Good tuition helps identify those gaps and gives them proper attention.

At Edgbaston Tuition Centre, we teach GCSE Maths in small groups with experienced tutors.

Book a free consultation. We’ll get an idea of where your child is, what they need to work on and how we can help.

### Medium

**If your child is struggling with GCSE Maths, doing more Maths isn't always the answer.**

It depends what they're struggling with.

A student who doesn't understand algebra needs something different from one who understands it but makes mistakes under exam pressure. And a student with gaps going back several years probably doesn't need another pile of GCSE practice papers just yet.

That’s why it helps to work out what’s actually happening first.

At Edgbaston Tuition Centre, we identify strengths and gaps, build the understanding underneath the method, then work towards application, recall and exam performance.

Small groups mean our tutors can pay attention to the individual student rather than simply moving everyone through the same work.

Book a free consultation with us. We’ll talk about where your child is with Maths, what might be holding them back and what we’d recommend from there.

### Long

**Two students getting the same GCSE Maths grade can need completely different help.**

One might understand the Maths but lose marks through rushed working and poor exam technique.

Another might have learned enough methods to get by, but still have gaps in fractions, algebra or number skills underneath them.

Give both students the same stack of practice papers and you'll probably get two very different results.

That’s why we think the useful question at the beginning of tuition is quite simple:

What does *this* student actually need?

By GCSE, most students also know which parts of Maths they prefer. Left to revise alone, they'll naturally spend a little more time on the things they can do.

Fair enough. Getting questions right feels better than repeatedly confronting the thing you don't understand.

A tutor can be useful precisely because they don't have the same preference.

They can look across the mistakes, spot the pattern and decide whether a student needs an explanation, more practice, better recall or simply more experience applying what they know under exam conditions.

That’s how we approach GCSE Maths at Edgbaston Tuition Centre.

We assess, strengthen, challenge, practise and track progress in small groups with experienced tutors.

Not every child needs the same intervention. So we don't think the conversation should start by pretending they do.

Book a free consultation with us. We’ll get an idea of where your child currently is, talk through what they’re finding difficult and explain how we could support them.

## 2. Learn Arabic

### Short

**Learning to read Arabic well takes regular, corrected practice.**

Children need repetition, but they also need somebody listening closely enough to catch the things they're repeatedly getting wrong.

Over time, you start noticing the difference: fewer hesitations, less guessing and more of the reading becoming natural.

We teach Arabic in small groups at Edgbaston Tuition Centre with experienced teachers.

Book a free consultation and we can talk about where your child is with Arabic and the support they need next.

### Medium

**A child can know the Arabic alphabet and still find reading Arabic difficult.**

Knowing the individual pieces is one stage. Reading them accurately and comfortably together is another.

That transition takes practice.

And importantly, *corrected* practice.

Because children can repeat mistakes too. A teacher needs to hear where they're hesitating, what they're confusing and when they're guessing rather than reading.

As those things are corrected and practised, something nice starts happening. The child needs less help with things that once stopped them every time.

At Edgbaston Tuition Centre, children learn Arabic in small groups with experienced teachers, including our Arabic & Qur’an lead, an Al-Azhar graduate with more than 12 years’ teaching experience.

Book a free consultation with us. We’ll talk about your child’s current level, where they need support and how we can help them progress.

### Long

**Children learn Arabic at different speeds, and they don't all get stuck in the same places.**

One child might know the letters but struggle when they're joined.

Another reads reasonably well until they meet something unfamiliar.

Another has become quick enough that they're now guessing at words rather than properly reading them.

Those differences matter.

Because “more practice” is only useful when the practice is helping with the right thing.

Parents who regularly listen to their children read often notice the smaller signs of progress.

A combination of letters that used to cause a pause no longer does.

A correction doesn't need to be made every time.

The child stops waiting for help and works something out themselves.

That's often how fluency develops. Not through one dramatic breakthrough, but through lots of little things becoming easier.

Good teaching helps that happen deliberately.

A teacher can listen, correct, explain and then give a child enough opportunities to get something right themselves. Eventually, you want the teacher to be needed less for something that once required help every time.

At Edgbaston Tuition Centre, Arabic and Qur’an education are taught in small groups with experienced teachers. Our Arabic & Qur’an lead is an Al-Azhar graduate with more than 12 years’ teaching experience.

If you're considering Arabic tuition for your child, start with a free consultation.

We can get an idea of where they currently are, what they need help with and whether our teaching is the right fit for them.

## 3. 11+ Preparation

### Short

**11+ preparation needs to cover more than the subjects on the paper.**

Children also need to learn how to use what they know when they're working against the clock.

When to keep going. When to move on. How to recover after a difficult question.

Our 11+ preparation combines Maths, English, Verbal and Non-Verbal Reasoning with timed practice and mock exams.

Book a free consultation and we’ll look at where your child is now and what their preparation needs next.

### Medium

**Getting an 11+ question wrong doesn't always mean a child doesn't know how to do it.**

Sometimes the knowledge is missing.

Sometimes they misread the question. Sometimes they know exactly what to do but spend too long doing it. Sometimes one difficult section throws them off for the next one.

Those are different problems.

And once preparation is underway, understanding *why* marks are being lost becomes just as useful as knowing the score at the top of the paper.

Our 11+ preparation covers Maths, English, Verbal Reasoning and Non-Verbal Reasoning, alongside timed practice, exam technique and mock exams.

The aim is to work on what each child actually needs rather than simply giving them more papers to complete.

Book a free consultation at Edgbaston Tuition Centre. We’ll talk through where your child is in their preparation, what needs attention and how we can help.

### Long

**A good 11+ score doesn't come from knowledge alone.**

A child has to use that knowledge within a limited amount of time, across several different kinds of question, without letting the difficult ones consume the whole paper.

That changes what useful preparation looks like.

Of course children need to know the Maths and English. They need experience with Verbal and Non-Verbal Reasoning too.

But as preparation progresses, there’s another layer worth paying attention to.

*How are the marks actually being lost?*

A wrong answer might reveal a gap in knowledge.

Or the child might have understood everything and rushed the final step.

They might repeatedly misread a certain type of instruction.

They might spend four minutes fighting for one mark while easier questions sit unanswered further down the paper.

Same result on the page. Different thing to work on next.

That’s why practice papers are useful for more than producing scores.

They give a good teacher information.

At Edgbaston Tuition Centre, we use that information to help shape the preparation. We cover Maths, English, Verbal Reasoning and Non-Verbal Reasoning, alongside exam technique, timed practice and mock exams.

We want children to know more, certainly.

We also want them to become better at showing what they know when the paper is in front of them.

If you're considering 11+ tuition, book a free consultation with us. We’ll get an idea of your child’s current level and preparation, identify where support may be useful and talk you through how we can help.

## 4. Islamic Studies

### Short

**Islamic Studies should help a child understand their faith, not only remember information about it.**

Remembering matters. But as children grow, you also want them to explain things in their own words, ask questions and understand how what they're learning relates to their lives.

That’s the kind of relationship with knowledge we want to encourage.

If you're looking for Islamic Studies for your child, book a free consultation with Edgbaston Tuition Centre. We can talk about what you're looking for and how we can help.

### Medium

**There’s a difference between remembering an Islamic teaching and understanding what it means.**

Children need both.

Knowledge has to be learned. Names, events, principles and teachings matter. But eventually you also want a child to explain an idea without simply repeating the exact words they were given.

That's when you start seeing the knowledge becoming theirs.

They ask better questions. Make connections. Recognise how something they've learned relates to their behaviour or something happening outside the lesson.

At Edgbaston Tuition Centre, our approach to education is rooted in Islamic values, with knowledge, character, discipline, respect and curiosity belonging together.

We want children to learn properly and understand what they're learning.

If you're considering Islamic Studies for your child, book a free consultation. We can talk about what you want from their Islamic education and explain how we can support them.

### Long

**Islamic education needs to give children knowledge they can eventually understand for themselves.**

At first, a lot of learning necessarily involves being told things.

A child learns the answer. Remembers a name. Repeats a teaching. Learns what they should and shouldn't do.

That foundation matters.

But children grow.

And eventually, being able to give the right answer when a teacher asks isn't quite the same as understanding the answer when nobody has asked the question.

You start looking for something else.

Can they explain it in their own words?

Can they connect one thing they've learned to another?

Do they ask questions?

Does something from a lesson occasionally appear in a conversation three days later because they've been thinking about it?

Those are interesting moments because the knowledge is starting to become theirs.

That's part of how we think about education at Edgbaston Tuition Centre.

Our approach is rooted in Islamic values, and we talk about knowledge and character together deliberately.

Children don't develop their academic ability in one room, their character in another and their understanding of Islam somewhere completely separate.

It's the same child.

So while remembering and repetition have an important place in learning, we also want children to understand, question appropriately, make connections and gradually recognise what their knowledge means for the way they live.

That's a longer educational project than simply teaching the correct answer.

We think it's a worthwhile one.

If you're looking for Islamic Studies for your child, start with a free consultation. We can learn a little about your child, understand what you're looking for from their Islamic education and talk through how we may be able to help.
