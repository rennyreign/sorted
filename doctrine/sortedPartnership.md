# Sorted Partner Sales Doctrine

This doctrine is the operating guide for people selling through the Sorted partnership route.

Sorted Partners are not traditional closers. The role is to identify businesses that would benefit from better digital presentation, introduce them to Sorted, and keep the conversation calm, useful, and honest.

The product may begin with a website. The outcome Sorted sells is business modernisation.

---

## Core Principle

**We do not sell. We show.**

Most agencies ask a business owner to imagine what a better website could look like. Sorted reverses that cycle.

We create the concept first, show the work, and then let the owner decide whether the direction feels right for their business.

This matters because pressure reduces trust. Showing the work creates a more natural conversation:

- The owner can react to something real
- The partner does not need to oversell
- The value is visible before commitment
- The conversation starts with usefulness, not persuasion

People rarely buy “a website” in isolation. They buy confidence that their business can look more credible, win more enquiries, and convert more customers.

---

## What Sorted Improves

Every sales conversation should connect back to at least one of these three outcomes.

### 1. Trust

Before someone becomes a customer, they decide whether the business feels credible.

Sorted improves trust through:

- Modern, professional website design
- Stronger branding and presentation
- Customer reviews and social proof
- Clearer messaging
- Better imagery and content structure
- A more consistent digital presence

When a business looks competent online, customers feel safer choosing it.

### 2. Enquiries

Trust creates interest, but the business still needs to make contact easy.

Sorted improves enquiries by making businesses:

- Easier to understand
- Easier to contact
- Easier to request a quote from
- Easier to book
- Easier to start a conversation with

A website should do more than look good. It should move people towards action.

### 3. Customers

More enquiries only matter if they turn into paying customers.

Sorted improves conversion through:

- Clear calls to action
- Stronger positioning
- Simpler buying journeys
- Better offers and service framing
- Less friction across the customer experience

The goal is not more website visitors. The goal is more customers.

---

## The Partner Role

The partner’s job is to open doors, not force decisions.

A Sorted Partner is responsible for:

- Finding businesses with visible improvement potential
- Checking whether Sorted could genuinely help
- Introducing the business to a finished concept or clear opportunity
- Creating a relaxed conversation
- Passing qualified interest back into Sorted

A partner should not:

- Criticise the business or embarrass the owner
- Promise pricing, timelines, features, or results beyond confirmed Sorted offers
- Pressure someone into a call
- Present Sorted as a generic website agency
- Push operational improvement before the website has earned trust

The best partner behaves like a useful scout: observant, direct, respectful, and commercially aware.

## Ideal Customer

The best prospects are business owners who take pride in their work but whose digital presence no longer reflects the quality of the business they have built.

Typical fit:

- Trades and home improvement companies
- Gyms, fitness studios, and martial arts schools
- Clinics, dentists, and healthcare providers
- Professional services
- Restaurants, cafes, and hospitality venues
- Property and accommodation businesses
- Manufacturers and specialist local suppliers
- Local retailers
- Automotive businesses

The core signal:

**Their business is better than their website.**

Good prospects usually have visible proof that the business is active and valuable: reviews, real customers, local reputation, strong services, or clear evidence of demand.

Weak prospects usually have no clear offer, no proof of trade, no reachable decision maker, or a business that is not ready to invest attention in improvement.

## Sales Motion

Sorted sales is intentionally simple.

### 1. Identify

Find businesses whose online presence could be improved in a way a normal customer would notice.

Look for:

- Outdated design
- Confusing navigation
- Weak mobile experience
- Poor calls to action
- Missing reviews or proof
- Hard-to-find contact routes
- Good business reputation with poor digital presentation

### 2. Review

Ask one question before moving forward:

**Could Sorted genuinely make this business easier to trust, contact, or buy from?**

If the answer is no, do not force the lead.

### 3. Create

Sorted produces a personalised website concept or clear improvement direction.

The concept should make the opportunity visible without requiring the owner to imagine the result.

### 4. Introduce

Lead with the work, not the pitch.

Example:

> Hi James, we spent a little time redesigning your website because we thought your business deserved something more modern. Before explaining anything, I thought I would simply let you see it. We would love to hear what you think.

Keep the message short, specific, and respectful.

### 5. Conversation

If the owner is interested, arrange a short conversation to understand the business, answer questions, and discuss the design.

The purpose of the call is not to corner the owner. The purpose is to confirm whether the concept matches a real business need.

### 6. Agreement

If the business sees value, Sorted handles the project conversation, scope, and delivery path.

The partner should keep expectations clean and make sure the owner understands the next step.

## Discovery Questions

Good discovery is curiosity, not interrogation.

Use questions like:

- Where do most new customers come from right now?
- What do you want someone to do when they hit the site?
- Does the website make the business look as good as it is?
- What do people need to see before they call, book, or visit?
- What should customers know that is hard to find online?
- If the site worked harder, what would you want more of?

Listen for business pain, not just design preferences.

---

## Objection Handling

Objections are not battles. They tell you what the owner is worried about.

### “We already have a website.”

Say:

> Good. The question is whether it is pulling its weight. Does it help people trust you, contact you, and choose you?

If they are still happy, respect it and move on.

### “We are not looking to spend right now.”

Say:

> That is fair. We can show you the direction first. If it is not useful, you do not need to take it further.

Do not argue about budget before they have seen value.

### “We get enough work already.”

Say:

> That is a good position to be in. A stronger site can still bring in better-fit enquiries and save you answering the same questions every week.

Keep the point practical. Better does not always mean more; sometimes it means better-fit.

### “We had a bad experience before.”

Say:

> That is exactly why we show the work first. You see the direction before you commit to anything.

Stay calm. Do not defend the industry. Show why Sorted works differently.

## Partner Standards

Every Sorted Partner should be:

- Professional
- Friendly
- Curious
- Helpful
- Honest
- Respectful of people’s time

Never criticise a business. Never embarrass an owner about their current website. Never create false urgency.

The standard is simple:

**Leave the business owner with more clarity than they had before the conversation.**

---

## Boundaries

Partners may explain the Sorted model, introduce the concept, and guide the owner towards the next conversation.

Partners must escalate to Sorted before confirming:

- Final prices
- Delivery dates
- Feature commitments
- Hosting or CMS arrangements
- Contract terms
- Operational improvement work beyond the website
- Any claim about guaranteed leads, revenue, or rankings

Keep promises narrow and accurate.
