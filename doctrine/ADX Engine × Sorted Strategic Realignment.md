# Who Sorted Serves

Sorted serves **small businesses that are already generating revenue.**

This is not a segment choice. It is a qualification rule.

A business without revenue has aspirations. A business with revenue has operations — real customers, real enquiries, real follow-up, real admin, real hours being burned on routines that a machine should be doing.

**Revenue is the signal that operational pain is real and worth paying to remove.**

It tells us three things at once:

1. **Pain exists.** Transactions create repetitive work — quoting, booking, replying, reminding, chasing, reactivating. No transactions, nothing to automate.
2. **Pain is felt.** The owner is already time-poor. They are not shopping for technology. They are shopping for hours back.
3. **Pain is fundable.** A revenue-generating business can pay for a solution. A pre-revenue business cannot.

We do not chase startups. We do not chase hobbyists. We do not chase businesses that "might need a website one day."

We chase businesses that are already running and already feeling the cost of running manually.

---

# The Shift

We stop selling websites.

Not because websites disappear. Because a website becomes **one operator** among many.

```
Before:   Website  →  Sale

After:    Business Review
              ↓
          Operational Friction
              ↓
          Operator Recommendation
              ↓
          Website (if required)
```

Website discussions are subjective. Business discussions are objective. Nobody argues with saving five hours, reducing admin, eliminating missed enquiries, or collecting reviews automatically.

The website becomes infrastructure. Not the headline.

# The Hierarchy

Everything begins with Operations.


The previous three pillars — Trust, Enquiries, Customers — are retained, but redefined as **outcomes**, not products. They are what happens when the operations layer is fixed. They are not things we sell.

# Prospect Finder

The Prospect Finder no longer looks for businesses needing websites.

It looks for businesses **experiencing repetitive work** — and the strongest signal for that is an established, revenue-generating business showing signs of manual overhead.

| Business signal            | Operator opportunity     |
|----------------------------|--------------------------|
| High review count          | Review automation        |
| Emergency / on-call trades | Missed-call operator     |
| Trades quoting manually    | Quote follow-up          |
| Dentists / clinics         | Appointment reminders    |
| Gyms                       | Membership automation    |
| Restaurants                | Bookings                 |
| Estate agents              | Lead routing             |

The qualifying filter across all of these: **is this business already transacting?** If yes, the operational pain is real and the operator is sellable. If no, walk away.

# Internal Language

**Avoid:** agency, creative, website company, digital agency, marketing company.

**Use:** operators, operational improvement, repetitive work, systems, execution, infrastructure, business operations, operational modernisation.

The words we use internally shape the offer we present externally. If we call ourselves a website company, we will be quoted against website companies. If we call ourselves an operational improvement platform, we are quoted against the cost of not changing.
